## What actually separates the best mold remediation companies from the rest?

The best mold remediation companies hold IICRC AMRT (Applied Microbial Remediation Technician) certification, physically contain the work area with poly sheeting and negative air pressure, document the job with photos and samples, and bring in a third party, not their own technician, to confirm the air is clear before calling it finished.

Containment is the part homeowners skip over in their head but shouldn't. Mold remediation done to the [ANSI/IICRC S520](https://iicrc.org/s520/) standard means the work area gets physically sealed off from the rest of the house before anyone starts cutting into drywall. Without that, a crew can spread spores through the HVAC system or down the hallway while they're removing the contaminated material, and you end up with a bigger problem than you started with.

Ask to see the containment plan before work starts, not after. A company that can describe it in two sentences, where the plastic goes, how negative air pressure gets pulled, where the HEPA filtration vents, probably does it on every job. A company that gets vague is probably improvising.

## Does the company test for mold, or just remove it?

Those are two different services, and the best mold remediation companies are clear about which one they're performing on your job. Testing (air sampling, surface swabs, moisture mapping) identifies what's there and how far it spread; remediation is the physical removal and treatment.

The EPA doesn't set a numeric "safe" level for mold spores in a home ([EPA: Mold Testing or Sampling](https://www.epa.gov/mold/mold-testing-or-sampling)), so testing before the job scopes the extent of the problem, and testing after confirms the work actually brought levels back down to what's normal for that building. A company that skips pre-testing and just starts tearing out drywall based on a visual look is guessing at scope, which usually means you either overpay for unnecessary demo or they miss hidden growth behind a wall.

The more important question is who does the clearance test when the job is done. A company confirming its own work with its own technician has an obvious conflict of interest. The better practice, and what you should ask for, is an independent lab or a separate testing-only firm handling the before-and-after samples. ## Will they fix the moisture problem, or only treat the mold you can see?

Mold remediation without fixing the moisture source is a temporary fix. Mold needs water to grow, and most strains can start colonizing a damp surface within 24 to 48 hours ([EPA: A Brief Guide to Mold, Moisture and Your Home](https://www.epa.gov/mold)), so if the leak, the humidity, or the grading problem that caused it isn't addressed, the mold comes back.

Some companies only do mold removal and hand you off to a separate plumber or waterproofing contractor for the actual source repair. That's not disqualifying on its own, but it does mean two invoices, two schedules, and nobody fully accountable if the mold returns in six months. A company that handles both the moisture repair and the mold remediation under one written scope, or that works hand-in-glove with a specific trade partner they name upfront, removes that gap. ## What questions should you ask before hiring any of them?

Five questions separate a company that'll do the job right from one that's guessing: certification, containment, who tests, who fixes the source, and what's in writing.

1. Are your technicians IICRC AMRT certified, and can I see it?
2. Will the work area be contained per the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard, and how?
3. Who performs the clearance test when you're done, you or an independent lab?
4. Will you identify and document the moisture source, or is that a separate call?
5. What exactly is in the written scope, and what's excluded?

Any legitimate mold remediation company in the Shenango Valley should answer all five without hesitation.
