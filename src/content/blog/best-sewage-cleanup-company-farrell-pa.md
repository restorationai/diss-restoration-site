## What makes a sewage backup different from a regular water loss?

A sewage backup is almost always Category 3 water, also called black water, which means it is grossly contaminated and unsafe to treat like a clean-water leak. Any backup from a floor drain, a toilet overflow with feces, or a sewer line beyond the trap falls into this category under the [ANSI/IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration](https://iicrc.org/s500/).

That classification changes the whole job. Porous materials like carpet pad, baseboard, and often drywall below the waterline typically get removed rather than dried in place, because Category 3 water carries bacteria and pathogens that don't come out with a wet vac and a fan. Crews working a real Category 3 loss wear Tyvek suits, gloves, and respirators, and the space gets an antimicrobial treatment before it's considered safe to rebuild.

This is also why a company's PPE and containment practices matter more here than almost any other service category. The [CDC's guidance on cleaning safely after a disaster](https://www.cdc.gov/natural-disasters/safety/index.html) specifically calls out sewage-contaminated water as a higher-risk cleanup than ordinary flooding.
