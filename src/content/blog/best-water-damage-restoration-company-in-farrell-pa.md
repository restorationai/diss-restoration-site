## What should you check before hiring a water damage company in Farrell?

Check four things before you sign anything: IICRC certification, licensing and insurance, how fast they can actually get a technician on-site, and whether they document the loss the way your insurer expects.

IICRC certification means a company's technicians have trained against the [ANSI/IICRC S500 Standard for Professional Water Damage Restoration](https://iicrc.org/s500/), the industry benchmark for how water categories, drying classes, and equipment placement are supposed to work. A company without it may still do fine work, but you have no independent standard to hold them to. Ask to see the certification, not just a logo on a website.

Licensing and insurance protect you if a drying job goes wrong or a worker is hurt in your home. In Farrell's older steel-worker housing stock, with original plaster, knob-and-tube wiring remnants, and cast-iron drain lines, a company also needs to know when a water loss intersects with lead paint rules, since many of these homes predate 1978.
