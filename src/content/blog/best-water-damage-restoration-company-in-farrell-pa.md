## What Should You Check Before Hiring a Water Damage Company in Farrell?

Three things matter most: IICRC certification, proof of license and insurance, and a willingness to put the scope of work in writing before starting. A company drying your home should be certified to the [ANSI/IICRC S500 standard](https://iicrc.org/s500/), the industry benchmark for water damage restoration procedures.

Ask whether the crew documents moisture readings and photographs as they go. That documentation is what your insurance adjuster will want to see, and it's also how you confirm the drying actually worked before anyone closes up the walls. A company that can't describe its drying verification process in plain terms is one to keep asking questions of.

Also ask directly whether they offer 24/7 response or standard business hours only. Water damage doesn't wait for 9 a.m., and knowing which kind of company you're calling before the emergency happens saves time you won't have later.

## What Happens When You Call for Water Damage Restoration?

A water damage call typically starts with a phone assessment, followed by an on-site inspection with a moisture meter and thermal imaging to map how far the water traveled. From there, the crew extracts standing water, sets up air movers and dehumidifiers, and monitors drying over several days until moisture readings return to normal for the materials involved.

Throughout the job, a reputable company documents everything: photos, moisture logs, and a written scope of work, which becomes the paper trail your insurance claim relies on. If porous materials like drywall or carpet pad were contaminated by gray or black water under the [IICRC S500](https://iicrc.org/s500/) standard, those materials typically get removed rather than dried in place.
