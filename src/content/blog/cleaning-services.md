## How Is Air Duct Cleaning Different From Other Cleaning Services?

Air duct cleaning physically removes dust, debris, and allergens from inside the HVAC system itself, not just the visible surfaces of a room.

A standard cleaning crew vacuums floors and wipes counters. Duct cleaning uses a rotary brush or air-whip tool to agitate debris inside the ductwork, paired with a HEPA vacuum pulling negative pressure through the system so loosened particles don't just blow into the living space. The [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq) notes that duct cleaning is most clearly worth doing when there's visible mold growth, heavy dust or debris, or a known pest infestation inside the ducts, rather than on a fixed annual schedule. Technicians trained to NADCA and [IICRC](https://iicrc.org) standards also clean the supply and return registers, the air handler, and sometimes the coil, since those are the parts that actually drive indoor air quality.

This matters more in homes with older HVAC systems, which is common in the Farrell and Shenango Valley housing stock, where ductwork can be decades old and was never cleaned during previous ownership. It also matters right after any renovation, since fine drywall dust settles directly into the return air system and recirculates through the house for months if it's never cleaned out.
