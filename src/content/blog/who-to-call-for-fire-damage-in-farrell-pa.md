## Who do I call first after a house fire in Farrell?

Call a fire damage restoration company first, right after the fire department clears you to approach the property. DISS Restoration is based in Farrell and takes calls around the clock, so the board-up and the first drying and odor-control steps can start the same night instead of waiting until morning.

That timing matters because an open roof or a broken window left unsecured overnight invites rain, animals, and theft on top of fire damage you're already dealing with. A restoration crew's first job is stopping the damage from getting worse, not filing paperwork.

## When do I call someone other than a restoration company?

Call 911 if there's any active fire, smoke re-ignition, or a structure that looks like it could collapse. Call the gas or electric utility directly if you smell gas or see downed lines near the house. Call your insurance company once the property is secured and mitigation has started, not as your very first call.

Fire departments in the Shenango Valley will often shut off gas and electric service to a damaged home before they leave. Don't try to restore either yourself. If the utility hasn't already disconnected service and you smell gas, leave the area and call from somewhere else.

Your insurance company is important, but calling them before the property is boarded up or protected can cost you time you don't have. Most policies also put the duty to prevent further damage on the homeowner, so getting a restoration company moving first actually supports the claim rather than working around it.

## What happens when you call for fire damage help?

The first call is a short triage conversation: what burned, how large the area is, whether the home is currently occupiable, and whether utilities are still on. From there, the crew prioritizes securing the structure before anything else.

Board-up and roof tarping come first if windows, doors, or the roof were breached, since an open structure is the fastest way for a bad situation to get worse. From there the work typically splits into two tracks: smoke and soot cleanup on surfaces and contents that can be saved, and structural repair or [reconstruction](/services/reconstruction/) for anything burned, charred, or compromised beyond cleaning.

Smoke odor is its own problem separate from visible soot. Smoke particles work into drywall, insulation, and HVAC ductwork, which is why wiping down visible soot without treating the air and the materials underneath rarely gets rid of the smell. Professional [smoke damage restoration](/services/smoke-damage-restoration/) uses HEPA air scrubbing, sealing, and sometimes thermal fogging matched to the type of smoke the fire produced, following the process outlined in the [ANSI/IICRC S700 Standard for Professional Fire and Smoke Damage Restoration](https://iicrc.org/s700/). If furniture, electronics, or clothing can be salvaged, a pack-out moves them offsite for cleaning while the structural work happens, which also keeps contents out of the way of dust and demolition.

## How do I document fire damage for my insurance claim?

Photograph every damaged room and item before anything is moved or cleaned, and keep every receipt tied to the fire, from the hotel stay to the board-up invoice. Most homeowners policies cover sudden fire loss, but the payout depends heavily on documentation filed early, not after repairs are underway.

The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/article/what-is-covered-by-standard-homeowners-insurance) notes that standard homeowners policies generally cover fire damage, including smoke damage and often temporary living expenses while the home is unlivable, though specifics vary by carrier and policy. A written scope of damage from the restoration company, before demolition starts, gives the adjuster something concrete to work from instead of relying on memory. The [U.S. Fire Administration's guide on what to do after a home fire](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/after-a-fire/) is a useful checklist for the first 24 to 48 hours, covering everything from securing valuables to contacting your mortgage company.
