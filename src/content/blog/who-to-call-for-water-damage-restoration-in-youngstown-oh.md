But "restoration company" is not always the first call. Here is how to triage:

- **If water is still flowing from a burst pipe or failed appliance line**: Call a licensed plumber first to shut off the source. Then call DISS Restoration. Extraction and drying cannot begin until the water is off.
- **If there is an electrical hazard** (outlets submerged, breaker panel in standing water, sparking anywhere): Do not enter the space. Call 911 or your utility company (Ohio Edison or FirstEnergy for most of Youngstown) to cut power before anyone goes in.
- **If you smell gas**: Leave immediately. Call 911 and Columbia Gas of Ohio from outside the building. Do not flip switches or use your phone inside.
- **If the water source is already stopped** (a pipe that already burst and drained, a washing machine that overflowed and stopped, rain that has passed): Call DISS Restoration at (724) 981-1441 directly. This is the most common scenario, and the sooner extraction starts, the less secondary damage you face.

Once the source is controlled and the scene is safe, DISS Restoration is your next call and your primary point of contact through the rest of the process, including with your insurer.

## When Should I Call My Insurance Company?

Call your insurance company after mitigation has started, not before. Most adjusters will tell you the same thing: stop the damage first, document everything, then file. Waiting for an adjuster before calling a restoration company can turn a $4,000 water loss into a $15,000 mold and structural claim.

Here is the practical sequence:

1. Make the property safe (shut off water, address electrical hazards).
2. Call DISS Restoration at (724) 981-1441. Their crew documents the loss with moisture readings, photos, and a written scope before any work begins.
3. Notify your insurer and give them the restoration company's contact information. DISS Restoration works directly with insurance carriers and can provide the documentation adjusters need.
4. Do not throw anything away before the adjuster or restoration crew has documented it. Damaged contents are part of your claim.

## What Happens When You Call DISS Restoration?

When you call (724) 981-1441, you reach a live person any time of day or night, not a voicemail. Here is what to expect:

**On the call**, a technician will ask you a few quick questions: Where is the water? Is the source stopped? Is there any electrical concern? Is there visible sewage or contamination? These answers determine how the crew stages their equipment and PPE before arrival.

**On arrival**, the crew performs a moisture assessment using thermal imaging and calibrated moisture meters to map the full extent of saturation, including inside walls and under flooring where damage is not visible. This step matters because water travels. A leak at a second-floor bathroom can saturate a first-floor ceiling, subfloor, and wall cavities before you see a single drip.

**Extraction comes next.** Truck-mounted extractors remove standing water far faster than shop vacs or consumer-grade pumps. For a flooded basement in a Youngstown-area home, this phase typically takes one to three hours depending on volume.

**Structural drying follows.** The crew deploys LGR (low-grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers and high-velocity air movers in a calculated pattern based on the [IICRC S500 standard](https://www.iicrc.org) for water damage restoration. Drying typically takes three to five days for a Category 1 (clean water) loss in standard residential construction. Category 2 (gray water, such as a dishwasher or washing machine overflow) or Category 3 (sewage or groundwater) losses require additional sanitizing steps and longer dry times.

**Monitoring continues daily.** A technician returns each day to take moisture readings and adjust equipment placement. Drying is not done when the floor looks dry. It is done when the moisture content of the structural materials returns to acceptable levels, documented with calibrated readings.

## Does Water Damage Always Lead to Mold?

Not always, but the window is short. The [EPA recommends](https://www.epa.gov/mold) beginning drying within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth. In Youngstown's humid summers and wet falls, that window can be even tighter in poorly ventilated spaces like basements and crawl spaces.

If extraction and drying begin promptly and moisture levels are brought down to acceptable ranges before mold can colonize, remediation is not needed. If drying is delayed or incomplete, mold can establish in as little as 24 to 48 hours on wet drywall, insulation, and wood framing. That is why the first call matters so much.

DISS Restoration holds IICRC AMRT (Applied Microbial Remediation Technician) certification, so if mold is discovered during the drying process, the same company handles it without handing you off to a third party.
