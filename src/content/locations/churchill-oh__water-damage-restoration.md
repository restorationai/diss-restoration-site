## Why Churchill Properties See Water Damage Issues

The freeze-thaw swings that move through the Mahoning Valley each winter are hard on old plumbing. Pipes that run through unheated crawlspaces or exterior block walls contract and expand with every cold snap, and a lot of Churchill's original copper and galvanized lines are past the point where a small stress crack turns into a full split overnight. Add in sump pumps that haven't been tested since the last heavy spring rain, and you get the two most common calls we get from this part of the county: a frozen-then-burst supply line and a failed sump during snowmelt.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Churchill

We start with extraction, pulling standing water out of carpet, pad, and any low spots in a finished basement before it has a chance to wick further up the drywall. From there we check moisture content in the framing and subfloor with a meter rather than guessing by feel, because water under older hardwood or behind block veneer can read dry on the surface while the material underneath is still saturated. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 guidelines: air movers positioned to create cross-flow, dehumidifiers sized to the cubic footage of the space, and daily moisture checks until the readings hold steady. On block foundation homes, we pay close attention to the wall cavity itself, since masonry releases trapped moisture more slowly than drywall and can re-wet a room if drying is cut short.
