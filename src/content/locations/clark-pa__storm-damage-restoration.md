## Why Clark Properties See Storm Damage

Clark sits in the Shenango Valley region of western Pennsylvania, where weather moves fast off Lake Erie and the surrounding ridgelines funnel wind gusts that can snap limbs off the large oaks and maples shading many of the borough's older homes. Housing stock here skews toward mid-century construction with asphalt shingle roofs that have often been reroofed once or twice, meaning flashing and underlayment don't always match current code. When a storm peels back shingles or drives a branch through a roof deck, water tends to travel along rafters and ceiling joists before it ever shows up as a stain, so damage is usually bigger than what's visible from the attic hatch. Older electrical services in this part of Mercer County can also be vulnerable to surges or outages during severe weather, which matters when sump pumps and dehumidifiers need power to keep ahead of incoming water.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Clark

We start with a roof and exterior assessment to find where wind or impact damage opened the building envelope, then install tarps or board-up material to stop ongoing intrusion before anything else happens. Inside, we extract standing water, pull saturated insulation and drywall that won't dry in place, and set up air movers and dehumidifiers calibrated to the room's moisture load rather than a one-size-fits-all setup. Drying is tracked against the IICRC S500 standard with daily moisture readings, so you have documentation showing the structure actually reached a safe moisture level before we close the job out, not just that equipment ran for a set number of days. For homes with downed trees across a roof or driveway, we coordinate tree removal ahead of the drying phase so crews aren't working around a hazard.

## Clark Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most storm claims in this area come through homeowners' wind, hail, or named-storm coverage, and adjusters want to see the damage documented before repairs start, not after. We photograph roof penetrations, siding damage, and interior water intrusion as we find it, and keep moisture logs that carriers typically request to support a claim. If a fallen tree is involved, we document whether it came from the insured property or a neighboring lot, since that detail often affects which policy responds first.

## Local Note

A lot of Clark's older homes still have knob-and-tube or early-generation wiring in the attic space, which means a roof breach that lets water track along the rafters can bring it into direct contact with wiring runs most newer homes don't have up there anymore. We flag that during the initial walk-through and loop in an electrician before running drying equipment in that part of the attic, rather than assuming it's safe the way we would in a home built after a full rewire.
