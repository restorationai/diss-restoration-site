## Why Clark Properties See Water Damage Issues

Clark sits in the heart of Mercer County's old steel corridor, and a lot of the housing here was built in the early-to-mid 1900s to serve workers at the mills that once ran along the Shenango River valley. That means stone and block foundations, galvanized plumbing nearing or past its service life, and basements that were never designed with modern waterproofing membranes. Combine that with western Pennsylvania's freeze-thaw winters and heavy spring runoff, and you get two recurring failure points: pipes that split when temperatures swing hard overnight, and basements that take on groundwater once the frost line shifts and saturated soil has nowhere to go but in through old mortar joints.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Clark

The first step is always extraction, pulling standing water off floors and out of carpet pad before it migrates further into subfloor and framing. In homes with the stone or block foundations typical of this area, we pay close attention to the wall-to-floor joint, since water tends to wick upward through porous masonry long after the visible puddle is gone. From there we set containment and begin structural drying per IICRC S500 guidelines, using moisture mapping to track progress in framing members, subfloor, and any plaster that's still original to the house. Older plaster walls hold moisture differently than drywall, they feel dry at the surface well before the lath behind them actually releases its water, so we extend monitoring in those rooms rather than pulling equipment on a fixed schedule.
