## Why Hilltop Properties See Asbestos Issues

A lot of the housing in and around Hilltop dates to the mid-20th century, when builders routinely used asbestos in joint compound, vinyl floor tile and the black mastic underneath it, pipe insulation, and the textured "popcorn" ceiling finish that was popular well into the 1980s. None of that is dangerous as long as it stays intact and undisturbed. The problem starts when a homeowner pulls up old flooring, scrapes a ceiling for a repaint, or a plumber cuts into wrapped pipe insulation during a repair. Fiber release happens fast once those materials are disturbed, and it's not something you can see happening in real time.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Hilltop

We start with testing, not assumptions. A technician collects samples from suspect material, bags them per EPA protocol, and sends them to an accredited lab rather than guessing based on age or appearance. Once results come back, we scope the containment: negative air pressure, poly sheeting barriers, and decontamination chambers sized to the room or area involved, whether that's a single ceiling or a basement full of old pipe wrap. Removal follows OSHA-trained procedures for wetting material before disturbance to limit airborne fiber, double-bagging waste, and disposing of it through a licensed facility. We don't skip the paperwork trail; if your project needs documentation for a lender, buyer, or insurance claim, we keep records of testing and disposal manifests.

## Popcorn Ceilings and Older Flooring: What We See Most

The two most common calls we get from this part of Ohio are popcorn ceiling testing ahead of a remodel and old 9x9 or 12x12 floor tile that's cracking or lifting. Popcorn ceiling asbestos removal requires full containment of the room since the material is disturbed overhead and drifts down as fine dust. Floor tile is different: often the tile itself has low asbestos content, but the black mastic adhesive underneath is the bigger concern, and pulling tile without testing the adhesive first is a common mistake we see from DIY renovations gone sideways.

## Local note

One thing that trips up a lot of homeowners here: a cracked or water-stained popcorn ceiling in an older Hilltop home almost always needs testing before any plumber, electrician, or painter touches it, because even a small access hole cut for wiring or a fixture can disturb enough material to require containment. We've had jobs where the actual abatement area was small, but the room had to be sealed off entirely because nobody tested before the first cut was made.
