## Why Hilltop Properties See Mold Issues

Hilltop sits within the Mahoning Valley, where summer humidity lingers and winter freeze-thaw cycles stress foundations year after year. A lot of the housing here dates to the mid-20th century, poured concrete or block basements that were never built with vapor barriers, and those basements are exactly where we find the most persistent mold problems. Add in cast-iron and galvanized plumbing that's reaching the end of its service life in many of these homes, and you get slow, hidden leaks that feed mold for months before anyone notices a smell or a stain. Humid crawlspaces and homes with window units instead of central air (common in this part of Ohio) also run higher indoor humidity, which is often enough on its own to support surface mold on drywall and in closets.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in Hilltop

We start by finding the moisture source, not just the mold, because remediation that skips this step is a guaranteed repeat visit. Our IICRC AMRT-trained technicians set up physical containment with negative air pressure before any demolition begins, so spores don't migrate into rooms that were clean when we arrived. Affected porous materials (drywall, insulation, sometimes subflooring) get removed and bagged for disposal rather than "cleaned," while structural wood and framing are treated with an EPA-registered antimicrobial and dried per the IICRC S500 standard. We run air scrubbers and dehumidifiers through the containment zone until moisture readings in the affected materials are back to a normal range for the season, not just a flat number pulled from a chart. Once the space is dry and visibly clean, we do a final walkthrough with the homeowner before any rebuild work starts.

## Local note

A detail that catches a lot of Hilltop homeowners off guard: block foundation walls common in this area can wick groundwater through the porous concrete itself, not just through visible cracks, which means a basement can show mold growth along the base of the wall even when there's never been standing water on the floor. We check moisture content in the block itself during inspection, not just the framing and drywall, because treating the surface without addressing a wicking foundation wall means the mold comes right back.
