## Why Hilltop Properties See Storm Damage

Ohio's spring and summer storm pattern brings fast-moving thunderstorm lines capable of straight-line wind gusts and, on the worse end, tornado touchdowns, often with little warning beyond a few minutes. Hilltop sits in that same weather corridor as the rest of the Mahoning Valley region, which means roofs built for routine rain and snow load get tested by wind-driven rain and hail they weren't necessarily designed around. Mature tree canopy, common in older Ohio residential blocks, adds another variable: large limbs and whole trees coming down on roofs, fences, and power lines during high-wind events. Once a roof or window is breached, water intrusion starts immediately, and in humid Ohio summers, hidden moisture behind drywall or insulation can support mold growth within 24 to 48 hours if it isn't addressed.

## Our Storm Damage Restoration Process in Hilltop

The first visit is about stopping further damage, not starting the full rebuild. That means emergency tarping over open roof sections, board-up on broken windows or doors, and extraction of any standing water that's made it inside. From there we document everything with photos and moisture readings, both for your records and for the insurance claim. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard, with air movers and dehumidifiers placed according to how deep the moisture has traveled into framing, subfloor, or insulation, not just what's visible on the surface. Only after materials read dry do we move into repairs, whether that's roofing, drywall, flooring, or full reconstruction on more severe losses.
