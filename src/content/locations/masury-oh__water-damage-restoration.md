Masury sits close to the Shenango River in Trumbull County, and that proximity means a naturally high water table in low-lying sections of the community. Add in older galvanized or early-generation copper plumbing common in houses from that era, and you get a second failure point: pinhole leaks and pipe bursts that often go unnoticed behind walls until flooring starts to cup or a basement smell shows up. ## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in Masury

We start with moisture mapping, because what looks dry on the surface of a block foundation or a plaster-and-lath wall can still be saturated underneath. Water is extracted first with truck-mount or portable extraction units depending on how much volume we're dealing with, then we set up structural drying equipment calibrated to the specific materials in the home: older plaster holds and releases moisture much slower than modern drywall, so drying timelines get adjusted accordingly rather than following a generic schedule. We check subfloor and joist moisture content daily and document readings for the insurance file. Drying follows IICRC S500 standard practices, and we don't close out a job until readings confirm the structure has returned to a normal moisture range, not just a visually dry one.
