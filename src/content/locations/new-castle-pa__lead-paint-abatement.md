**Lead paint abatement in New Castle** comes with a wrinkle that doesn't show up in newer suburbs: the city's housing stock dates largely to the tin and steel boom of the early 1900s through the 1950s, which means layer after layer of lead-based paint on trim, sash, and plaster walls that have been repainted for decades without anyone testing what's underneath. If you're prepping a home for sale, a rental inspection, or a renovation permit in Lawrence County, that paint history determines how the job gets done, not just whether it gets done.

## Why New Castle Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Most of New Castle's housing predates the 1978 federal ban on residential lead paint by several decades, and a good share of it predates even that by another generation. Rowhouses and foursquares built for mill and foundry workers were painted and repainted on tight budgets, often with lead-based primers on window sashes, porches, and baseboards because that's what held up against coal soot and humidity off the Shenango River valley. When those surfaces are sanded, scraped, or disturbed during a kitchen remodel or window replacement, lead dust can settle into floor cracks and HVAC returns long before anyone notices a problem. Renovation contractors working in these older sections need to assume lead is present until testing proves otherwise, not the reverse.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in New Castle

We start with testing, not assumptions. A certified technician checks painted surfaces most likely to have been disturbed over the years: window and door trim, stair risers, exterior siding, and any area flagged for upcoming renovation work. Once we know what we're dealing with, we containment the work area with poly sheeting and negative air where needed, following EPA RRP lead-safe work practices rather than open demolition. Paint and dust are removed using wet methods and HEPA-filtered tools to keep lead particles from becoming airborne, and all waste is bagged and disposed of according to EPA guidelines rather than tossed in a standard dumpster. Final clearance includes a visual check and surface wipe testing before we release the space back for painting or renovation to continue.

## Equipment & Methods We Use for Lead Paint

HEPA vacuums and wet-scraping tools do most of the physical work here, since dry sanding is exactly what turns a manageable paint layer into an airborne hazard. For older plaster walls common in New Castle's pre-war housing stock, we favor chemical stripping or encapsulation over aggressive mechanical removal, since plaster is brittle and tends to crack or crumble under heavy scraping. Window units get special handling, too: the friction surfaces where sashes rub against the frame are where lead dust concentrates fastest, so those get tested and treated even when the rest of a room looks fine.

## Local note

A lot of New Castle's older homes were updated with replacement windows at some point without anyone touching the original trim or sill, which means the new vinyl window sits right next to decades-old painted wood that was never tested. We've learned to check that transition zone specifically, because it's an easy spot for a renovation crew or inspector to miss, and it's exactly where friction-generated lead dust tends to collect.
