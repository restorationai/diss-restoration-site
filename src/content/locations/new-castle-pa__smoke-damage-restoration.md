## Why New Castle Properties See Smoke Damage Issues

A lot of New Castle's housing predates 1950, built during the city's run as a steel and tinplate manufacturing center. Balloon framing was standard then, meaning there's often no fire-blocking between floors, just a continuous stud cavity from basement to attic. Smoke takes the path of least resistance, so even a contained kitchen fire can leave soot staining in a second-floor closet with no obvious damage. New Castle's reputation as the Fireworks Capital of America, tied to the Zambelli family's manufacturing history here, also means the area has a longer local memory of industrial and structure fires than most towns this size, and insurance adjusters here are used to seeing both puffback soot from older oil furnaces and wildfire-adjacent smoke drift during dry summer stretches along the Shenango River valley.

## Our Smoke Damage Restoration Process in New Castle

We start by mapping where the smoke actually traveled, not just where the fire was, which in a balloon-framed New Castle house usually means opening wall or attic access points most homeowners wouldn't think to check. From there the process is straightforward: HEPA vacuuming of loose soot, wet or dry sponge cleaning depending on the surface, thermal fogging or ozone treatment for odor bound into porous materials like plaster and old lath, and a final seal-and-paint step on anything the cleaning can't fully restore. Older homes here often still have original plaster walls, which hold onto smoke odor longer than drywall, so we budget extra time for odor-neutralizing passes rather than calling the job done after one treatment.
