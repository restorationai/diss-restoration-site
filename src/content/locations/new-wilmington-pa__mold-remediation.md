**Mold problem in New Wilmington?** Pennsylvania's freeze-thaw winters followed by humid summers create exactly the kind of temperature swings that drive condensation into wall cavities and crawlspaces, and once moisture sits for more than a day or two, colonization starts. DISS Restoration handles mold remediation for homes and small commercial buildings throughout New Wilmington and the surrounding Lawrence County area, working from an IICRC-trained process rather than guesswork.

## Why New Wilmington Properties See Mold Issues

New Wilmington's housing stock skews older, with a mix of early-to-mid-1900s frame homes near the borough center and newer construction further out toward the township line. Many of these older homes still have fieldstone or block foundations without modern vapor barriers, which means groundwater and seasonal runoff find their way into basements and crawlspaces more easily than in newer builds. Add in a college-town rental market, where units often sit vacant between semesters with HVAC systems powered down, and you get stagnant, humid air sitting undisturbed for weeks at a time. That combination, old foundations plus inconsistent heating and cooling cycles, is a pattern we see again and again in this part of Lawrence County.

## Our Mold Remediation Process in New Wilmington

We start with a visual and moisture-meter inspection to find both the mold and the moisture source feeding it, because removing growth without fixing the water intrusion just means it comes back. Containment goes up next: negative air pressure chambers with physical barriers to keep spores from migrating into unaffected rooms, especially important in the narrower hallways and finished basements common in New Wilmington's older homes. From there, our AMRT-trained crew removes and bags contaminated materials (drywall, insulation, sometimes subflooring), HEPA-vacuums and treats remaining surfaces, and runs air scrubbers through the drying period. We document every step with photos for your insurance file, since adjusters generally want to see the extent of removal before approving reconstruction costs.

## New Wilmington Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most homeowner's policies in Pennsylvania treat mold as an exclusion unless it stems from a covered peril, like a burst pipe or storm-driven roof leak, so the moisture source matters as much as the mold itself when it comes to what a carrier will pay. We photograph and document the originating cause (a failed sump pump, a roof leak around a chimney flashing, a slow plumbing leak under a slab) alongside the mold itself, which gives your adjuster a clearer basis for approving remediation and the resulting drywall or flooring replacement. If your property is managed through a landlord or a small rental association near Westminster College, we also coordinate scheduling around tenant turnover windows so the work doesn't collide with move-in or move-out dates.

## Local note

A detail that catches a lot of homeowners off guard here: New Wilmington's older crawlspaces, particularly in homes built before mid-century, often vent directly to the outside with no vapor barrier over the dirt floor. In summer, warm humid air meets the cooler soil underneath and condenses on floor joists, which is why we'll sometimes find mold on framing even when there's been no obvious leak. Sealing and encapsulating that crawlspace during remediation, rather than just treating the visible growth, is usually the difference between a one-time job and a repeat call a year later.
