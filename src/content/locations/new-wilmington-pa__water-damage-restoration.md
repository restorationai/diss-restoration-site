## Why New Wilmington Properties See Water Damage Issues

New Wilmington's housing stock skews older than much of the surrounding county, with a concentration of homes built in the late 1800s and early 1900s around the college and the borough core. Many of these properties still have original plaster-and-lath walls, galvanized supply lines, and basements that were never meant to handle modern washing machine or water heater failures. Galvanized pipe corrodes from the inside out, and pinhole leaks inside a wall cavity can go unnoticed for days before they show up as a stain on a ceiling. Western Pennsylvania's freeze-thaw winters add another layer of risk: ground that freezes and thaws repeatedly shifts foundations and stresses buried water lines, and a hard freeze followed by a quick warm-up is a common trigger for burst pipes in unheated crawlspaces and detached garages on the rural edges of town. Properties outside the borough center that rely on private wells and sump systems also see groundwater intrusion after heavy spring rain, when saturated soil has nowhere else to go but into the lowest point of the house.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in New Wilmington

We start with a moisture assessment using penetrating and non-penetrating meters to find out how far water has traveled, which matters more in plaster-wall homes than in newer drywall construction because plaster can look dry at the surface while the lath behind it stays wet. Extraction comes next, pulling standing water from flooring, subfloor, and basement slabs before it has a chance to wick further into framing. Once the bulk water is gone, we set up structural drying equipment calibrated to the materials involved, dried in line with the IICRC S500 standard rather than on a fixed timeline, because a plaster wall in a 1910 New Wilmington home dries at a different rate than drywall in a newer build on the edge of town. ## Equipment & Methods We Use for Water Damage

Water extraction relies on truck-mounted and portable extraction units depending on how deep the water is and where it's pooled, followed by low-grain refrigerant dehumidifiers and air movers positioned to pull moisture out of wall cavities and subfloor, not just off the surface.
