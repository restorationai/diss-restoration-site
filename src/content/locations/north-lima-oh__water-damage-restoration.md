## Why North Lima Properties See Water Damage Issues

North Lima sits in Springfield Township, where a lot of the housing stock is older farmhouses and ranch homes built with full basements or crawlspaces rather than slab foundations. Many of these properties still run on private wells and septic systems, and when a pressure tank fails or a line freezes during an Ohio cold snap, water can collect under the house for hours before anyone notices the pressure drop. The clay-heavy soil common across this part of Mahoning County also drains slowly, so a heavy spring thaw or a sump pump that can't keep pace tends to push groundwater right up against foundation walls. Combine that with decades-old cast iron or galvanized plumbing in some of the township's older homes, and small leaks become bigger problems fast.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in North Lima

We start with moisture mapping, checking not just visible wet flooring but what's happening behind baseboards, under vinyl or laminate, and inside wall cavities where older insulation can hold water long after the surface looks dry. Extraction comes next, pulling standing water out of basements and crawlspaces with truck-mounted or portable units depending on access. From there we set up structural drying equipment calibrated to the building, dense older framing and plaster dry slower than open-concept drywall construction, so we adjust air movers and dehumidifiers and track moisture content daily rather than guessing at a timeline. Drying follows IICRC S500 guidelines, and we document readings throughout so the numbers back up when the job is actually finished, not just when it looks dry.
