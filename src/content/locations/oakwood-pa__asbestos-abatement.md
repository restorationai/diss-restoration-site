DISS Restoration handles testing, containment, and removal so a simple ceiling update or basement project doesn't turn into an airborne hazard for your family or tenants.

## Why Oakwood Properties See Asbestos Issues

Oakwood's older homes, many built before the 1980s federal restrictions on asbestos-containing building materials, often still have the original textured "popcorn" ceiling finish, 9x9 vinyl floor tiles, and taped duct or pipe wrap in the basement or crawlspace. These materials were durable and cheap, which is exactly why builders across western Pennsylvania used them so widely. The risk isn't the material sitting undisturbed, it's what happens when a homeowner sands a ceiling, pulls up old flooring for a remodel, or a contractor cuts into a wall without testing first. Disturbed asbestos fibers don't settle back down on their own, and HVAC systems can pull them through the rest of the house.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Oakwood

We start with sampling, not assumptions. A small material sample goes to an accredited lab for analysis before we recommend any removal plan, because not every popcorn ceiling or floor tile in an Oakwood home actually contains asbestos, and testing is the only way to know. Once a positive result comes back, we isolate the work area with negative air pressure containment and poly sheeting, suit up in the respirators and disposable coveralls OSHA training requires, and remove the material wet to keep fibers from going airborne. Everything gets double-bagged and manifested for disposal at a facility licensed to accept asbestos waste, and we document the chain of custody the whole way so you have a paper trail if a future buyer's inspector or your insurance carrier asks for it.

## Local Note

A detail that catches a lot of Oakwood homeowners off guard: attic insulation. Blown-in vermiculite insulation, installed widely across western Pennsylvania through the 1970s, can carry asbestos fibers depending on its original source mine, and it looks almost identical whether it's contaminated or not. We don't assume either way. If a job involves attic access, rewiring, or spray foam retrofits over existing insulation, we test the vermiculite first instead of treating it as automatically safe just because it's not a ceiling or floor material.
