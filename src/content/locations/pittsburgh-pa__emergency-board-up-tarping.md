**Storm damage or a break-in just put a hole in your roof or a window in Pittsburgh?** Board-up and tarping needs to happen fast, before rain, wind, or freezing temperatures get further into the structure. We answer 24/7, and our crews bring plywood, tarps, and fasteners rated for the kind of wind-driven weather that rolls through the region, not a patch job that tears loose overnight.

Pittsburgh's housing stock makes emergency board-up a different job than it is in newer suburban markets. A huge share of the city's homes and small commercial buildings date to the early-to-mid 1900s, when the city's steel mills were running and rowhouses, brick duplexes, and slate-roofed structures went up fast and close together. That era of construction means irregular roof pitches, original wood-frame windows, and brick facades that were never built with modern tarping anchors in mind. When a window breaks or a section of roof decking fails, we're often working around balloon framing or old plaster that reacts badly to even short-term water exposure, so the board-up has to be tight the first time.

## Why Pittsburgh Properties See Board-Up and Tarping Emergencies

The city's hilly topography and position where the Allegheny, Monongahela, and Ohio Rivers meet create wind patterns that funnel through valleys and across exposed ridgelines, putting extra stress on roofing in neighborhoods built on steep grades. Add winters with repeated freeze-thaw cycles, and you get ice damming, cracked flashing, and wind-lifted shingles that turn into open roof penetrations almost overnight. Vacant or storm-damaged structures left exposed even briefly also become targets for secondary break-ins, which is its own reason to get plywood over a window or door fast rather than wait.

## Our Emergency Board-Up and Tarping Process in Pittsburgh

We start with a structural walk of the damaged area to confirm what's actually compromised versus what just looks bad, since older roof assemblies in this region can hide rot or prior patch jobs under newer shingles. For openings, we measure and cut exterior-grade plywood sized to the actual frame, not a generic sheet, because original window and door openings in pre-war Pittsburgh construction rarely match modern standard sizes. For roofs, we lay heavy-gauge tarp secured with batten strips and fasteners driven into solid decking, not just the shingle layer, so wind doesn't work the tarp loose during the next storm system. Every job gets photographed before and after for the insurance file.

## Pittsburgh Insurance & HOA Coordination

Most carriers treat emergency board-up and tarping as mitigation, meaning the cost of securing the property is typically covered separately from the repair estimate, and documenting that you acted quickly can support the larger claim. We provide photos, material lists, and a written scope that matches what adjusters expect to see for storm or fire-related openings. For properties in HOA-managed developments or historic districts, we also note when exterior material choices (like plywood color or visible tarping) may need a quick note to the association or local preservation guidelines, since some older Pittsburgh neighborhoods have review processes for exterior changes even temporary ones.

## Local note

One thing that catches people off guard here: a lot of Pittsburgh's older roofs were built with slate or built-up tar and gravel systems, both of which behave differently under a tarp than asphalt shingle roofs do. Slate edges can shatter further if a tarp is stretched and fastened the wrong way, and tar-and-gravel surfaces need the tarp secured without puncturing the membrane in spots that are still structurally sound. We check roof type before we ever cut a fastener, because the wrong anchoring method on either of these older systems can turn a temporary fix into a bigger repair bill.
