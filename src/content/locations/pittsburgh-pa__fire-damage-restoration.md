## Why Pittsburgh Properties See Fire Damage Issues

Pittsburgh's housing stock skews old. Entire blocks were built during the steel boom of the early 1900s, and a lot of that construction used balloon framing, a method where wall cavities run uninterrupted from basement to attic. That design was efficient to build but it also gives fire, smoke, and heat a clear path to travel between floors and even into an attached neighbor's unit before anyone smells smoke upstairs. Add in decades of electrical upgrades layered on top of original knob-and-tube wiring, plus long, cold winters that keep furnaces and space heaters running hard for months at a time, and you get a city where heating-related and electrical fires show up with real regularity. Hillside neighborhoods with narrow, winding streets can also slow fire department response and staging, which sometimes means more water and suppression damage by the time the fire is out.

## Our Fire Damage Restoration Process in Pittsburgh

Every fire job starts with a structural safety check, because a fire-weakened floor joist or a scorched load-bearing wall in a hundred-year-old home behaves differently than one in new construction. From there we document the loss for your adjuster, extract any standing water left by suppression efforts, and begin soot and odor removal. Masonry and plaster, both common in Pittsburgh's older homes, hold smoke odor longer than modern drywall, so we budget extra time for odor encapsulation and, when needed, thermal fogging to reach inside wall cavities that balloon framing leaves open. Once the structure is dry and stable, we move into reconstruction, matching trim, plaster, and masonry details where possible instead of defaulting to drywall everywhere.

## Local note

In Pittsburgh's older rowhouse blocks, a fire that looks contained to one unit often isn't. Because so many of these buildings share balloon-framed walls, smoke and heat can travel into the attached structure next door through the open stud cavity even when there's no visible flame damage on that side. We inspect adjoining units in these blocks as a matter of course, not as an upsell, because skipping that step on shared-wall construction is how hidden smoke damage gets missed.
