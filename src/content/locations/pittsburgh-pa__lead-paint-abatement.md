**Lead paint hiding under decades of repainting in a Pittsburgh rowhouse?** With so much of the city's housing stock dating back to the steel boom of the early 1900s through the 1950s, lead-based paint is less an exception here than an expectation in pre-1978 construction. A failed remodel, a DIY scraping job, or even a simple window repair can release lead dust into a home faster than most owners realize, and the fix has to follow EPA Renovation, Repair, and Painting rules, not guesswork.

## Why Pittsburgh Properties See Lead Paint Issues

Pittsburgh's housing stock skews old. Entire blocks of rowhouses, duplexes, and single-family frame homes went up during the steel industry's peak, long before lead was pulled from residential paint in 1978. Add the city's hilly terrain and narrow lot lines, and you get houses with tight side yards, shared walls, and older wood trim, window sashes, and porch rails that were repainted layer over layer for decades without anyone stripping the original lead-based coats underneath. Freeze-thaw cycles common to western Pennsylvania winters also accelerate paint failure on exterior wood, which is often how lead dust first becomes airborne: not through a renovation, but through ordinary weathering and peeling.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Pittsburgh

We start with testing, because assuming a home is lead-free based on its age alone is a guess, not a fact. Once lead-based paint is confirmed, containment goes up around the work area using plastic sheeting and negative air pressure where the layout allows, which matters in Pittsburgh's older homes where rooms tend to be smaller and ventilation paths less predictable than in newer construction. Paint and substrate removal follows EPA RRP work practices, wet methods and HEPA-filtered tools to keep dust from migrating, with all debris bagged and disposed of as regulated waste. We finish with a HEPA vacuum pass and visual clearance before anyone moves back into the space, and we document the work for owners who need records for a sale, a refinance, or a rental inspection.

## Pittsburgh Insurance & HOA Coordination

Lead abatement doesn't typically run through a homeowner's insurance claim the way storm or water damage does, so most of this work is paid out of pocket or coordinated as part of a larger renovation budget. Where it does intersect with insurance is when lead paint is disturbed during a covered repair, like a fire or water loss in an older home, and remediation becomes part of getting the property back to a safe, code-compliant condition. For rental properties, we also work with landlords and property managers who need lead-safe documentation to satisfy Allegheny County health requirements or a buyer's lender before closing.

## Local note

A detail that trips up a lot of out-of-town contractors: Pittsburgh's older rowhouses and hillside lots often leave almost no side-yard clearance, which means standard containment tents and negative air setups have to be scaled down and anchored differently than they would be on a suburban lot with normal setbacks. We size our containment to the actual footprint of these homes rather than using a one-size approach, which keeps the work compliant without tearing up tight exterior spaces that owners still have to live with afterward.
