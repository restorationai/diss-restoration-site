**Finishing a renovation in Pittsburgh and staring at a layer of drywall dust on every surface?** Post-construction cleanup here is rarely as simple as a quick wipe-down. Between gypsum dust working into old plaster seams, saw residue settling into HVAC returns, and general contractors who leave a job "broom clean" at best, most Pittsburgh homeowners need a real final clean before move-in or listing. DISS Restoration handles the detail work a renovation crew doesn't: construction debris hauling, window track and vent cleaning, and the kind of deep clean that gets a space ready to actually live in.

## Why Pittsburgh Renovations Leave Behind More Than Drywall Dust

A large share of Pittsburgh's housing stock dates to the first half of the last century, built during the city's industrial growth years. Plaster walls, original trim, and older subfloors all behave differently under renovation than modern drywall-and-stud construction. When a wall gets opened or sanded in one of these homes, the dust isn't just gypsum, it can carry old paint residue, and pre-1978 construction means lead is a real possibility even on a routine update. That changes how a final clean has to be handled, not just how thorough it has to be. Add in Pittsburgh's steep, narrow streets and alley-style lots in older parts of the city, and even getting a debris trailer or dumpster staged close to the house takes more planning than it would on a flat suburban lot.

## Our Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning Process

We start with a walkthrough to flag anything that needs special handling, old paint layers, plaster dust, leftover adhesive, before any cleaning begins. From there the process generally runs:

- Coarse debris and material haul-away, sorted for disposal or recycling
- HEPA vacuuming of all horizontal and vertical surfaces, including ceiling lines and light fixtures
- Detail cleaning of window tracks, baseboards, cabinet interiors, and vents, where fine dust collects long after a crew calls a job done
- HVAC register and return cleaning, since construction dust pulled into the system will recirculate for months if it's not addressed
- Final detail pass: glass, fixtures, flooring, so the space is genuinely move-in or show-ready, not just visually clean

For homes with suspected lead-containing materials, we follow EPA Lead-Safe work practices for containment and cleanup, which matters more in Pittsburgh's older housing stock than it would in a newer build.

## Equipment & Methods We Use

Standard shop vacuums push fine construction dust back into the air instead of removing it, which is why we run HEPA-filtered extraction on drywall dust, sanding residue, and plaster debris. On jobs involving older painted surfaces, our crews are OSHA trained and follow EPA Lead-Safe containment and cleanup protocols rather than treating the dust like ordinary household dirt. For larger renovation and addition projects, that same equipment handles everything from sawdust in HVAC returns to the fine residue that settles on flooring after tile or stone work.

## Local note

Pittsburgh's older rowhouses and steel-era single-family homes were often built with lath-and-plaster walls, and renovation dust on these surfaces settles into tiny cracks and seams that newer drywall simply doesn't have. A final clean on one of these homes usually takes a second detail pass on trim and corners that a standard post-construction clean would skip on a newer build. It's a small difference, but it's the reason a "final clean" quote for a 1920s Pittsburgh two-story and a 2015 build in the same square footage won't look identical.
