Asbestos turns up more often in Sharon than in newer suburbs, and the reason is simple: most of the housing stock here predates the 1980 phase-out. If you're pulling up old floor tile in a Shenango River valley bungalow or scraping a popcorn ceiling in a house built when Sharon Steel and Westinghouse were still hiring by the thousands, there's a real chance you're looking at asbestos-containing material, not just dust.

## Why Sharon's Older Housing Stock Raises Asbestos Concerns

Sharon grew up around the steel industry, and a huge share of its homes and commercial buildings date from the early-to-mid 1900s through the 1960s. That era of construction leaned heavily on asbestos: it's in troweled and spray-applied popcorn ceilings, 9x9 vinyl floor tile and the black mastic underneath it, pipe wrap and boiler insulation in basements, and sometimes in old HVAC duct seams. Mercer County's housing inventory skews older than a lot of the region, which means renovation and demolition projects here run into these materials more frequently than they would in a subdivision built after 1990. Disturbing any of it with a sander, a heat gun, or a pry bar is how a routine remodel turns into an air-quality problem.

## Our Asbestos Abatement Process in Sharon

We start with testing, not assumptions. A sample of suspect material goes to a lab before anyone touches drywall, flooring, or insulation, because visual inspection alone can't tell you whether a ceiling texture or tile adhesive is asbestos-containing. If the result comes back positive, we isolate the work area with poly sheeting and negative air pressure, use HEPA-filtered vacuums and wet methods to keep fibers from going airborne, and bag and label material for disposal per EPA and Pennsylvania DEP requirements. Our crew works under EPA Lead-Safe Certified and OSHA-trained protocols, which matters in Sharon specifically because so many of these jobs involve homes where lead paint and asbestos insulation show up in the same renovation. We don't skip steps to speed things up: containment and clearance are what keep a family from moving back into a house with fiber still in the air.

## Local note

A lot of the two-story frame houses built in Sharon between the 1920s and 1950s have vermiculite insulation in the attic, which can contain asbestos depending on the source mine it came from. Homeowners doing attic work or adding insulation on top of existing material sometimes don't realize what they're disturbing until dust is already coming down through the ceiling fixtures below. If you're planning an attic project in one of these older Sharon homes, it's worth having the insulation tested before you touch it, not after.
