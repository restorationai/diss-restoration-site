## Why South New Castle Properties See Water Damage

Lawrence County's freeze-thaw winters are hard on older plumbing. Galvanized supply lines common in homes built through the mid-20th century corrode from the inside and tend to fail without warning, usually in a wall cavity or crawlspace where nobody notices until a ceiling stain or a spongy floor gives it away. Add in the borough's proximity to the Shenango River and its tributaries, and you get a second risk factor: heavy regional rainfall pushes groundwater up through foundation walls even in homes that have never flooded from the surface. Basements with stone or block foundations, which are common in this part of the county, are especially prone to wicking moisture through mortar joints during sustained wet stretches.

## Our Water Damage Restoration Process in South New Castle

We start with extraction, pulling standing water out with truck-mounted or portable pumps depending on how the property is accessed, then move into moisture mapping. In older block-foundation basements, we check behind and beneath finished walls early, since masonry holds moisture longer than drywall and can mask a problem that looks dry on the surface. Structural drying follows the IICRC S500 standard: we set air movers and dehumidifiers based on room volume and material type, not a flat number of machines per square foot, and we track moisture content daily until subfloor, framing, and masonry all read dry, not just the top layer. For homes with finished basements, we document everything that has to come out, flooring, baseboard, sometimes lower drywall, before demolition starts, so the insurance paperwork matches what actually happened.
