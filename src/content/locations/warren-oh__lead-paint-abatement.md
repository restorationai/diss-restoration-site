**Lead paint in a home you're renovating in Warren?** Steel-era housing stock across the city means a lot of pre-1978 paint layers, and disturbing them during a remodel or window replacement without proper containment can spread contaminated dust through an entire house in a single afternoon.

Warren's housing grew up alongside the steel and manufacturing boom of the early-to-mid 1900s, and that era shows up on almost every block. Layered additions, replacement windows installed over original lead-primed sash, and repainted porches and trim are common, which means a visual check rarely tells the whole story. A surface coat from the 1990s can sit directly over three or four older coats that test positive, and sanding, scraping, or even heat-gun work on those layers is enough to trigger EPA RRP requirements.

## Why Warren's Housing Stock Raises Lead Paint Risk

Most of the older housing in and around Warren predates the 1978 federal lead paint ban by decades, which puts window casings, porch rails, baseboards, and exterior siding squarely in the risk window. Trumbull County's older neighborhoods also tend to have original wood-frame construction with painted exterior trim that's been recoated repeatedly rather than stripped, so each repaint adds another layer that can contain lead. Combine that with Ohio's freeze-thaw winters, which crack and chip exterior paint faster than milder climates, and you get more friable paint dust generated from normal weathering alone, not just renovation work.

## Our Lead Paint Abatement Process in Warren

We start with EPA RRP-compliant testing on suspect surfaces, paying close attention to window units, door frames, and any trim that's been through multiple repaints, since that's where Warren homes most often hide older coats. Once we've confirmed lead content, we seal off the work area with containment plastic and negative air pressure before any scraping, sanding, or demolition begins. Debris is wet-misted to keep dust down, bagged as hazardous waste per EPA protocol, and the space is HEPA-vacuumed and wipe-tested before we call the area clear. For homes where full removal isn't practical, we also handle encapsulation, which seals lead paint under a specialized coating rather than disturbing it.

## Local note

One thing that trips up a lot of Warren homeowners doing their own prep work: a single paint chip test on a porch rail or window sash isn't reliable in homes from the steel-boom era, because the layer that tests negative on top is often sitting over two or three older coats that test positive. We test at multiple depths on any surface that's been repainted more than once, which is standard here given how common repainted trim is on Warren's older housing stock.
