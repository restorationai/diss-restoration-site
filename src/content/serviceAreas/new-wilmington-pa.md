## Restoration emergencies common in New Wilmington

New Wilmington sits in Lawrence County's farm and small-town corridor, where winters bring hard freeze-thaw cycles that split older galvanized supply lines and crack foundation walls. Spring thaw and heavy summer storms push groundwater against basement walls built decades before modern waterproofing membranes were standard, and that pressure finds its way in through mortar joints and old tile drain systems. Many of the borough's homes and farmhouses date back far enough that a single burst pipe or roof leak can sit inside plaster walls or dirt-floor crawlspaces for days before anyone notices the smell. That lag is exactly what lets mold colonize and what turns a contained leak into a structural repair.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in New Wilmington

Much of New Wilmington's housing stock predates mid-century construction standards, with balloon-framed walls, plaster-and-lath interiors, and a mix of full basements and dirt or stone crawlspaces that hold moisture longer than modern poured foundations. Plumbing in these homes often still includes galvanized or early copper supply lines and cast iron drains, both of which are prone to pinhole leaks and corrosion failure as they age. Older homes in the borough may also contain asbestos-containing floor tile, pipe insulation, or lead-based paint, which changes how a restoration scope is handled once materials are opened up.

Lawrence County's clay-heavy soil holds water close to the surface after heavy rain, adding hydrostatic pressure against older basement walls that weren't built with modern drainage boards or sump systems. Structural repairs and any rebuild work typically require a permit through the borough or county building authority, particularly when framing, electrical, or plumbing systems are affected, so we plan our scope with that review in mind rather than treating it as an afterthought.
