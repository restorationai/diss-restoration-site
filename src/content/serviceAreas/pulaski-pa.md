## Restoration emergencies common in Pulaski

Pulaski Township sits in a largely rural stretch of Lawrence County, where many properties run on well water, septic systems, and older rural electrical service rather than municipal utilities. That combination means a failed sump pump, a frozen well line, or a septic backup can turn into standing water in a basement or crawlspace before anyone notices, especially on properties where the nearest neighbor is a field away. Winters here bring hard freezes that split exposed or poorly insulated plumbing runs in farmhouses and older ranch homes, and spring thaw combined with heavy rain pushes groundwater against foundations that were never built with modern drainage in mind. Severe summer thunderstorms roll through the region too, bringing wind damage to roofs and outbuildings that were built for an earlier, calmer set of expectations.

## Services we provide in Pulaski

Water damage restoration in Pulaski often starts below ground: fieldstone or block basements that take on water through old mortar joints, or crawlspaces under additions that were never properly vented. Mold remediation becomes a concern whenever a water loss sits unaddressed, particularly in enclosed crawlspaces and basements common to this township's older housing stock. We contain the affected area, remove compromised materials, and dry the space to stop further colonization.

Storm damage response covers wind-damaged roofing, downed tree limbs through roof decking, and the tarping and board-up work that keeps a property protected while repairs are scheduled.

## Building stock, site conditions, and permits in Pulaski

Much of Pulaski Township's housing dates from the mid-20th century, with a mix of wood-frame farmhouses, ranch-style homes on crawlspaces or partial basements, and scattered newer construction. Older homes in the township commonly still carry galvanized supply lines or early copper, and drain lines that have seen decades of mineral buildup, both of which raise the odds of a slow leak going unnoticed until it reaches flooring or framing. Homes built before the 1980s may also contain asbestos in flooring, siding, or pipe insulation, which changes how a restoration scope has to be handled once walls or flooring are opened up.

Much of the township relies on private wells and septic systems rather than municipal water and sewer, which affects both the cause and the cleanup approach for water losses. Lawrence County issues permits for structural repair and rebuild work in unincorporated portions of the township, and any project that touches load-bearing framing or significant electrical and plumbing work will typically need to go through that process before work closes up the walls.
