**Water spraying from a wall or ceiling, a sudden drop in water pressure, or a utility bill that spiked for no reason?** These are the first signs of a burst pipe, and the clock starts the moment it fails. We answer 24/7, and every minute between the break and the shutoff valve is more water into your subfloor, drywall, and insulation.

A burst pipe is different from a slow leak. It's a full-volume failure, often from freeze expansion, corroded galvanized supply lines, or a joint that finally gave out under water hammer pressure. In Farrell's older housing stock, a lot of original plumbing is past its intended service life, and a hard freeze or a sudden pressure surge is enough to split a fitting that's been quietly weakening for years.

## What burst pipe cleanup and repair actually involves

The job has two halves that have to happen almost simultaneously: stopping the water source and removing the water that's already loose in the structure. We isolate the line at the nearest shutoff, then move into extraction while the plumbing side gets assessed. Depending on where the break occurred (slab, wall cavity, ceiling joist bay, or crawlspace), the demolition needed to access and repair the pipe varies a lot. A pipe burst inside a finished wall usually means opening drywall to both reach the break and check for water that's already tracked down into the wall cavity, often much farther from the visible damage than homeowners expect.

Equipment on a typical burst pipe call includes submersible pumps for standing water, truck-mount extraction for saturated carpet and pad, air movers and dehumidifiers for the structural drying phase, and moisture meters to map how far the water traveled behind finished surfaces. If the water sat for more than 24 to 48 hours or came from a source other than clean supply water, the category of loss changes what can be dried in place versus what has to be removed.

## Our process

1. **Shutoff and source control.** We confirm the water is off at the main or the fixture isolation valve before anything else starts. Active pipe break water damage only gets worse while water keeps flowing.
2. **Extraction.** Standing water comes out first, from flooring, wall cavities where accessible, and any low points where it pooled.
3. **Demolition for access.** Where the break is inside a wall, ceiling, or slab penetration, we open only what's needed to expose the pipe and the wet materials behind it.
4. **Pipe repair coordination.** We document the break for insurance and coordinate the physical pipe repair, whether that's a section replacement, a repair coupling, or a re-route.
5. **Structural drying and moisture monitoring.** Air movers and dehumidifiers go in, and we track moisture content in framing and subfloor daily until readings return to dry standard, not just until the surface looks dry.

## What separates a good burst pipe response from a bad one

The most common mistake on a burst pipe emergency is drying the visible area and closing the wall back up before checking what's behind it. Water that enters at a ceiling joist can travel several feet along the joist before dropping down into a wall cavity on the other side of the room. A moisture meter and, where warranted, a thermal camera are what catch that, not a visual check.

The second mistake is treating every burst pipe the same regardless of what was in the water. A clean supply line break is a very different job than a drain line or sewage-adjacent pipe failure, and the materials that have to be removed versus dried change accordingly. Insurance adjusters are specifically looking for documented moisture readings at the start and end of drying, photos of the affected cavity before it's closed back up, and a clear record that the water category was assessed, not assumed.

## What does burst pipe cleanup and repair cost?

Costs vary by how much water escaped before the shutoff, how far it traveled into the structure, and whether finished materials like drywall, flooring, or cabinetry need to be removed and rebuilt. Every loss gets a written scope before work begins, since no two burst pipe calls have the same amount of exposed water or hidden travel. Homeowners insurance typically covers sudden and accidental water damage from a pipe failure, though coverage can be affected by how long the pipe was leaking before discovery or whether the home was unoccupied.

## Seasonal & regional considerations

Farrell sees real winter freeze-thaw cycles, and that's when pipe failures spike, particularly in uninsulated crawlspaces, exterior-wall supply lines, and homes where heat gets set back or shut off during vacancy. A pipe doesn't have to freeze solid to fail: the expansion that happens as water freezes inside a confined fitting is often enough to split copper or crack a soldered joint well before the whole line is frozen. Water line break calls also pick up when temperatures swing back above freezing fast, since that's when a line that cracked overnight starts spraying as pressure returns.
