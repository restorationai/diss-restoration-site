Smoke that's soaked into drywall after a kitchen fire, a musty basement smell that keeps coming back no matter how many candles you light, pet odor that's worked its way into subfloor, or the lingering smell of standing water that's long gone but the stink hasn't followed: these are different chemical problems with different fixes, and masking them with air fresheners only buys a few hours before the odor resurfaces. Odor removal and deodonization in Farrell means identifying what's actually holding the smell in the structure and treating it at the source, not spraying over it.

## What Odor Removal and Deodorization actually involves

Odor isn't a smell floating in the air, it's particles and gases that have bonded to porous surfaces: drywall, subflooring, insulation, upholstery fabric, even grout lines. Surface cleaning rarely reaches it. Our technicians use a combination of thermal fogging, which carries deodorizing agents into the same tiny spaces smoke or moisture originally penetrated, hydroxyl generators for occupied spaces where ozone isn't safe to run, and ozone treatment for unoccupied structures where a stronger oxidizing reaction is appropriate. Treatment length depends on how deep the odor has set in: a single room with light smoke exposure might clear in a day, while a whole-house fire odor job or a long-standing mold or sewage smell can take multiple treatment cycles over several days, with source materials like insulation or carpet padding removed first when they're too saturated to treat in place.

## Our process

1. **Source identification.** We trace the odor to its origin, whether that's charred framing behind drywall, water-damaged subfloor, or fabric that's absorbed smoke particulate, because treating the air without addressing the source is a temporary fix at best.
2. **Removal of non-salvageable materials.** Porous materials that have absorbed odor beyond what treatment can reverse (soaked insulation, saturated carpet padding, heavily charred wood) get removed rather than masked.
3. **Thermal fogging or ozone/hydroxyl treatment.** Depending on the structure, occupancy, and odor type, we select fogging to penetrate crevices and seams, or ozone/hydroxyl generation to neutralize airborne and surface-bound odor molecules over a set dwell time.
4. **Sealing and encapsulation where needed.** Framing or subfloor with persistent smoke odor sometimes needs an odor-blocking sealant applied before reconstruction continues.
5. **Verification.** We re-check treated areas, including returning after the dwell period for ozone jobs, to confirm the odor hasn't rebounded before calling the job finished.

## What separates a good odor removal response from a bad one

The most common mistake is treating the air and skipping the materials. Spraying an enzyme deodorizer across a room that still has smoke-saturated drywall or a urine-soaked subfloor will smell fine for a week and then come right back, especially with humidity swings. A thorough technician identifies which materials are salvageable and which need to come out, rather than defaulting to the cheapest option of fogging everything and hoping.

Ozone is also frequently misused. It's effective in unoccupied spaces over controlled dwell times, but it's not something that should be run with people, pets, or plants in the building, and overuse can damage rubber, certain plastics, and some finishes. A technician trained under IICRC FSRT standards for fire and smoke know when ozone is appropriate versus when hydroxyl generation or a different approach is the safer call. Insurance adjusters reviewing odor claims typically want documentation of which materials were removed, which were treated in place, and what equipment ran for how long, so thorough photo documentation and a clear scope matter as much as the treatment itself.

## What does Odor Removal and Deodorization cost?

Cost depends heavily on what's driving the odor and how deep it's set into the structure, not just the square footage of the room. A quick fogging treatment for light cooking or pet odor costs far less than a whole-house job involving material removal after a fire. Every loss is different, and DISS Restoration provides a written scope before any work begins so there are no surprises. Homeowners insurance typically covers odor remediation when it's tied to a covered peril like fire or a plumbing failure, but odor from long-term neglect or untreated moisture issues is usually excluded.

Farrell's humid summers and older housing stock mean basements and crawlspaces hold onto musty odor longer than drier climates, especially in homes with block foundations that wick moisture seasonally.
