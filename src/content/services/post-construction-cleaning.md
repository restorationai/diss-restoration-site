Drywall dust settles into HVAC returns, paint overspray dots the windows, and a film of sawdust and joint compound sits on every horizontal surface long after the trade crews pack up their tools. A general house cleaning service isn't built for this. Post-construction cleanup requires different equipment, different sequencing, and an understanding of which residues are safe to wipe and which need to be HEPA-vacuumed before they get airborne again.

## What Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning actually involves

This service covers the gap between "construction finished" and "move-in ready." That includes fine dust removal from every surface (not just the obvious ones: window tracks, light fixtures, vent covers, baseboards), adhesive and paint overspray removal from flooring and glass, interior window and sill detailing, and a final pass that gets a space to show-ready condition. On larger renovation or new-build jobs, it also covers debris staging and haul-off coordination so the final clean isn't fighting leftover material.

The equipment matters more than most homeowners expect. Standard vacuums recirculate fine particulate right back into the air; HEPA-filtered extraction keeps drywall dust from resettling an hour after the crew leaves. Microfiber and specific solvent choices matter too: the wrong cleaner on new hardwood finish or fresh paint can dull or streak a surface that took weeks to install correctly. Timeline runs from a half-day for a single remodeled bathroom to several days for a full-house renovation or new construction, depending on square footage and how much fine dust penetrated into cabinetry and HVAC components.

## Our process

1. **Walkthrough and scope** - we assess dust load, surface types, and any damaged or at-risk finishes before touching anything, and flag areas that need extra care (natural stone, unsealed wood, new paint).
2. **Dry removal first** - HEPA vacuuming and dry dusting of every surface from ceiling to floor, because wiping dust before it's lifted just grinds grit into finishes.
3. **Detail cleaning** - window tracks, light fixtures, vents, cabinet interiors, baseboards, and fixtures get wiped and degreased; adhesive residue, paint spatter, and label glue get worked off glass and flooring with finish-safe methods.
4. **Floor and surface finishing** - final mopping, polishing, or buffing appropriate to the material, done last so earlier dust passes don't undo it.
5. **Final walkthrough** - a room-by-room check against the scope, with touch-ups before we call it complete.

## What separates a good post-construction cleaning response from a bad one

The most common mistake is cleaning top to bottom but skipping the places dust actually hides: inside cabinet boxes, on top of door frames, behind appliances, and inside HVAC return grilles. If the system ran during construction, dust can be circulating through ductwork long after the visible mess is gone, which shows up as a gray film reappearing days later. A rushed crew also tends to use generic all-purpose cleaner on everything, which can leave streaking on new paint or haze on fresh polyurethane floors.

A careful crew tests cleaning solutions on an inconspicuous spot before working a whole surface, separates dry removal from wet cleaning so grit doesn't get dragged across finishes, and checks return vents and light fixture housings, not just the surfaces at eye level. On jobs tied to an insurance claim, like cleanup following a covered loss and repair, documentation of pre- and post-condition matters, since adjusters want to see that the finished space matches the scope of repair, not just that it looks clean.

## What does Post-Construction and Specialty Cleaning cost?

Cost depends on square footage, how much fine dust and debris is present, and whether the job is a single remodeled room or a full-house renovation. Every property is different, so DISS Restoration provides a written scope before work begins rather than a flat rate quoted over the phone. Homeowners insurance typically does not cover routine post-construction cleaning unless the renovation itself was triggered by a covered loss, such as fire or water damage repair, in which case the final clean may be included in that claim.
