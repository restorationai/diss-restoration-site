**Sewage backup in your home or business?** Raw sewage is classified as Category 3 black water, meaning every minute it sits on flooring, baseboards, or drywall increases the risk of contamination spreading into subfloor and framing. We answer 24/7 and treat every sewage call as a biohazard event from the first phone call, not a standard water loss.

## What Sewage Cleanup and Sanitization actually involves

A sewage backup is never just a water problem. Whether it's a sewer line backup pushing waste up through a floor drain, a septic overflow saturating a crawlspace, or a toilet line failure that's been running for hours before anyone noticed, the water carries bacteria, viruses, and parasites that make standard extraction and drying unsafe on their own. Crews work in full PPE (gloves, respirators, boot covers, and in bad cases, full suits) and treat the loss as contaminated from the start. Porous materials that absorbed sewage, carpet pad, drywall below the flood line, baseboards, often can't be saved and have to come out rather than be dried in place. Equipment includes truck-mount extraction, antimicrobial and enzymatic treatments, and air scrubbers to manage odor and airborne particulate while the space dries. Depending on how long the backup sat and how far it traveled under flooring, a job can run from a single day for a contained bathroom event to over a week when subfloor and wall cavities are involved.

## Our process

1. **Contamination assessment.** We identify the source (sewer line, septic, plumbing fixture) and trace how far the black water traveled, including under cabinets, behind baseboards, and into wall cavities.
2. **Raw sewage removal.** Standing waste and contaminated water are extracted first, using pumps and truck-mount equipment rated for black water, before any drying equipment goes in.
3. **Removal of unsalvageable materials.** Carpet, pad, and affected drywall or insulation that contacted sewage are cut out and bagged for disposal, since sanitizing porous materials that have absorbed Category 3 water isn't reliable.
4. **Cleaning and sanitization.** Remaining hard surfaces, framing, and subfloor are cleaned with EPA-registered antimicrobial products and treated to address bacteria and odor at the source, not just mask it.
5. **Drying and verification.** Structural drying equipment runs per IICRC S500 guidelines, with moisture readings taken until the space reaches a dry standard before any reconstruction starts.

## What separates a good sewage cleanup response from a bad one

The most common mistake is trying to dry sewage-contaminated materials instead of removing them. Drying equipment can bring a wet drywall panel back to a normal moisture reading, but it doesn't remove the bacteria that soaked in, and insurance adjusters know to ask whether contaminated porous materials were actually removed or just dried and painted over. A thorough response also documents the extent of contamination with photos and moisture mapping before demolition starts, since that documentation is what supports the claim later. Another gap is odor: if sanitization only addresses visible surfaces and skips the subfloor or wall cavity where waste traveled, the smell comes back within days as residual bacteria continues breaking down organic material. Good operators also test for cross-contamination into adjacent rooms via HVAC returns or flooring seams, something that's easy to miss on a quick walkthrough.
