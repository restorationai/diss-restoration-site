## What Water Damage Restoration actually involves

Water damage restoration is the combination of pulling standing water out of a structure and then drying the building materials that absorbed it, which is a separate and longer process than most homeowners expect. A flooded basement might look dry to the eye within an hour of extraction, but the concrete, framing, and insulation behind it can hold moisture for days. We use truck-mounted and portable extraction units to remove bulk water first, then set air movers and low-grain-refrigerant dehumidifiers to pull moisture out of wood, drywall, and subfloor. Category matters here: clean water from a supply line behaves very differently than water that's been sitting, or water that's come up through a floor drain or sewer backup, which is treated as contaminated and handled under stricter containment and disposal protocols.

## Our process

1. **Extraction.** We remove standing water with extraction equipment sized to the loss, from a single bathroom to a flooded basement, so drying can start immediately rather than after the water has had hours to spread further into materials.
2. **Structural drying.** Air movers and dehumidifiers are positioned based on psychrometric readings, not guesswork, targeting the wet building assemblies, subfloor, framing, and wall cavities, not just the visible surface.
3. **Moisture monitoring.** We take daily moisture readings in affected materials with penetrating and non-penetrating meters and log them, which documents when a material has actually reached dry standard rather than when it looks dry.
4. **Containment for Category 2/3 water.** When water is contaminated, from a sewage backup or water that's dwelled long enough to support bacterial growth, we isolate the affected area, use appropriate PPE, and follow disposal protocols for porous materials that can't be sanitized, like wet carpet pad or drywall.
5. **Reconstruction handoff.** Once materials test dry and the scope is complete, we document the finished drying process for your insurance file and hand off any needed rebuild work, flooring, drywall, trim, as its own phase.

## What separates a good water damage response from a bad one

The most common mistake is drying the surface and stopping. A wall can read dry with a surface moisture meter while the stud behind it, or the insulation inside the cavity, is still saturated. That trapped moisture is exactly what mold needs, and colonization can begin within 24 to 48 hours in favorable conditions. A thorough response pulls baseboards or drills small inspection holes to check wall cavities, moves or removes wet insulation rather than drying around it, and documents moisture readings over multiple days instead of declaring the job done after a single visit.

Insurance adjusters look for the same things: dated moisture logs, photos of the affected materials before demolition, and a clear category and class determination for the water loss, since that classification affects what the policy covers and how the drying plan is justified. Skipping documentation is one of the most common reasons a claim gets delayed or a scope gets disputed later.

## What does water damage restoration cost?

Costs depend on how much water intruded, how long it sat before extraction started, and what materials were affected.
